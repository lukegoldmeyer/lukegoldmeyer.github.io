## Background
Back in November 2025, my only computer solution was my Asus ROG Zephyrus M16 gaming laptop[^1]. It continues to be a great tool for mobile work, but at the time the keyboard was failing and I couldn't rely on it for everything, not to mention the sub-par performance when compared to a desktop. I decided to build a PC that fit my needs for reliability and for a performance upgrade, while fitting within my budget. Here's how I did it.

## The Build
Planning a PC build is one of my favorite parts of the process, and I used [pcpartpicker](https://pcpartpicker.com/) to make a few lists of potential builds, as well as Pinterest for inspiration. I remembered seeing a build at Micro Center that I thought looked very nice, and I decided to go for a fish tank style build. Lian Li makes some of the best fish tank cases, and their fans match the aesthetic very well, giving a sleek modern vibe with minimal RGB and good performance.

## Aesthetics and Cooling

I ended up choosing the [Lian Li O11 Vision Compact](https://lian-li.com/product/o11-vision-compact/) for my case, and Lian Li's [TL120 fans](https://lian-li.com/product/uni-fan-tl/), which are wireless and daisy-chainable. The O11 Vision Compact has a great amount of flexibility when it comes to configurations, and I chose to run the glass top panel, so I had room for eight fans in total. 

I was looking for an AIO cooler that performed well but also fit with the aesthetic, and the Lian Li Hydroshift II LCD-C came with three of the same TL120 fans I needed, so it was a no-brainer. I chose the 360mm radiator version, and the circular LCD screen is a nice touch for reading temps.

## Performance Parts

I side with Team Red for CPUs, so I picked the [Ryzen 9 9900X](https://www.amd.com/en/products/processors/desktops/ryzen/9000-series/amd-ryzen-9-9900x.html). It is a powerful 12-core, 24-thread desktop processor built for gaming and content creation. I get 4.4 GHz base clock and up to 5.6 GHz max boost, 64 MB of L3 cache and 12 MB of L2 cache (76 MB total). I also wanted to be on AM5 for some future proofing.

<Figure src={imageImg} alt="My Ryzen 9 9900X freshly installed in the motherboard" width={400} caption="My Ryzen 9 9900X freshly installed in the motherboard." size="small"/>

I tend to waver between Team Red (AMD) and Team Green (Nvidia) for GPUs (at the time of writing this), but for my build I decided to prioritize raster and raytracing performance over VRAM capacity. I chose the [RTX 5070 Ti](https://www.microcenter.com/product/690049/asus-nvidia-geforce-rtx-5070-ti-prime-triple-fan-16gb-gddr7-pcie-50-graphics-card), specifically the ASUS Prime OC. This card has 16GB of GDDR7 VRAM, and the OC Edition features a slightly higher boost clock (up to 2,527 MHz) when compared to other 5070 Ti's. The Prime OC also fits with the build's minimalist modern aesthetic, and it only takes up 2.5 slots.

<Figure src={asusPrimeRtx5070TiWithGradientBackground2Img} alt="The Asus PRIME 5070 Ti" width={1200} size="large" caption="The Asus PRIME 5070 Ti."/>

I hit the lottery when it came to memory timing, as I purchased the RAM _right before_ the AI memory price explosion of 2025. I ended up with 64GB of Corsair Vengeance RGB DDR5 at 6000mt/s which I got for around $250 the week before the price began to climb. That same kit is priced at around $1,300 at the time of writing. This capacity and speed allows me to open huge Solidworks assemblies and run projects in Blender and Unreal Engine with minimal hiccups.

Choosing a motherboard was simple, as I used one of Micro Center's fantastic bundles. I chose the [MSI MAG X870E Tomahawk WiFi](https://www.microcenter.com/product/690917/msi-x870e-mag-tomahawk-wifi-amd-am5-atx-motherboard), as it offers fantastic power delivery with a 14+2+1 phase Duet Rail Power System with 80A SPS and an 8-layer server-grade PCB. It also has four M.2 slots, two of which are gen 5. Overall it is a solid option with plenty of connectivity and support for powerful components.

I didn't cheap out on a power supply. I chose the [Lian Li Edge series 850W](https://lian-li.com/product/edge-gold/) 80 Plus Gold, which is a unique fully modular PSU in that the ports are turned 90 degrees for easy access. 

My PSU also came with nice looking textured cables and cable combs, which meant I didn't need to buy cable extensions. I was able to route them very cleanly and there is minimal mess in the back.
